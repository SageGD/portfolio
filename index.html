Cutting Costs for Copper Recycling with Sensor Fusion

Product Strategy Stakeholder Management ROI Analysis Requirements Definition

Led product strategy for AI-powered copper sorting platform targeting 30%+ operational cost reduction for industrial recyclers. Defined functional requirements, evaluated sensor technologies through ROI lens, and validated market fit with recycling partners. Collaborated with MIT engineers to scope MVP sensor configuration and deliver 88.75% classification accuracy.

Market Opportunity & Problem Statement

  • Problem: Manual copper sorting creates 30%+ operational costs and inconsistent quality leading to margin loss
  • Market: Industrial recyclers processing mixed scrap streams with thin profit margins
  • Solution hypothesis: Automated sensor fusion + AI can reduce labor dependency while improving accuracy
  • Success metric: 88.75% classification accuracy on high-grade copper (achieved through validation)

Stakeholder & User Research

  • Recyclers: Need faster throughput, reduced labor costs, higher grade separation accuracy
  • Sustainability stakeholders: Require scalable solution supporting circular economy
  • Engineering team: Need clear performance requirements and data collection frameworks
  • Conducted scrap yard site visits to understand operational constraints and pain points

Product Requirements & Technical Execution

  • Sensor stack: Spectroscopy, 3D depth camera, weight sensors, multiple visual cameras, object detection
  • System integration: Automated conveyor triggering sensors at detection points
  • ML pipeline: Multi-modal model trained on spectral, weight, geometry, and visual features
  • Performance: 88.75% accuracy on three-fold cross-validation for high-grade copper

Data-Driven Product Decisions

  • ROI analysis: Identified optical imaging as highest-value sensor for MVP
  • Feature prioritization: Reduced system complexity after data showed additional sensors decreased accuracy and increased cost
  • Root cause analysis: Paint/coatings identified as primary failure mode through error analysis
  • Roadmap recommendation: Add thermal, multispectral, and polarized imaging in V2 to address contamination

Business Impact & Validation

  • Demonstrated potential to reduce sorting labor costs and increase material value recovery
  • Validated product-market fit through industrial partner collaboration
  • Positioned solution in sustainable copper recycling infrastructure for green technology transition
  • Presented findings at MIT Energy Initiative Annual Research Conference

Product Management Skills Demonstrated

  • Scoped cost-effective MVP: optical + spectroscopy vs. full sensor suite
  • Balanced technical complexity with operational ROI and stakeholder constraints
  • Made build/buy decisions on sensor hardware based on performance and budget
  • Created product roadmap informed by failure mode analysis and market needs
  • Communicated technical tradeoffs to diverse stakeholders (engineers, business, sustainability)

Underwater Muon Detection System

Hardware Product Development Rapid Prototyping Constraint-Based Design Data Analysis

Led experimental physics project measuring cosmic ray muon attenuation in water using CosmicWatch detectors. Owned full product development lifecycle from requirements definition through deployment, delivering functional underwater detection system within budget and safety constraints. Demonstrated rapid iteration and pivot capabilities when initial design failed field testing.

Product Requirements & User Needs

  • Core requirement: Measure muon flux at depths 0-10m in lake environment
  • Constraints: Waterproof to 10m, lightweight enough for 2-person canoe deployment, <$500 budget
  • Safety critical: Must be retrievable from lake, stable enough to prevent capsizing
  • User need: Simple deployment/retrieval process operable in cold weather conditions
  • Data quality: 6-minute measurement windows at each depth with redundant waterproofing

Rapid Iteration Through Failure

  • V1 (Failed): IP68 pressure-rated container (35.6 x 35.6 x 18.3 cm) with tile ballast - too buoyant to sink despite passing sink test
  • V2 (Failed): Added lead bricks (27kg total) - solved buoyancy but created new problem: too heavy to retrieve from canoe
  • V3 (Shipped): Pivoted to lightweight dry bag + 2.3kg aluminum/stone ballast - achieved all requirements
  • Key learning: Validated assumptions early through field testing, not just controlled lab tests
  • Made kill-decision on sunk cost ($200+ housing investment) to deliver working product on timeline

Technical Problem-Solving

  • Waterproofing: Implemented defense-in-depth with nested Ziploc bags + dry bag (redundancy validated through testing)
  • Depth measurement: GPS coordinates + rope measurement system with 1.5m markings for precision
  • Detector optimization: Analyzed acceptance angle vs. count rate tradeoff, chose 2.5cm separation for 70% sky coverage
  • Power management: Validated 2-hour battery life with only 3% drain, eliminating mid-test recharging risk
  • Stability: Designed duct-taped ballast block to prevent dry bag puncture while maintaining vertical detector orientation

Data-Driven Decisions

  • Measured 70% muon attenuation at 9.75m depth, demonstrating exponential decay pattern
  • Identified systematic error: Sea-level measurement showed high angular velocity (40°/s) due to canoe motion
  • Root cause analysis: Experimental counts 55% lower than theoretical predictions, traced to wide acceptance angle (63.4°)
  • Validated detector separation experiment: 70% sky acceptance (d=2.5cm) vs. 35.6% (d=8cm) configuration
  • Quantified error sources: timing error <0.16%, angle error ~1%, temperature effects negligible

Product Management Outcomes

  • Delivered working underwater detector system within 6-week timeline and $500 budget
  • Successful deployment: 9 depth measurements (0-9.75m) with 2 trials each, zero equipment loss
  • Demonstrated rapid prototyping: 3 hardware iterations with fast fail-forward approach
  • Created reproducible methodology: Documented design for future student research teams
  • Balanced competing constraints: waterproofing, weight, cost, safety, data quality, and timeline

Future Roadmap Recommendations

  • Increase detector separation to 8cm to reduce acceptance angle and improve data accuracy
  • Add pressure sensor for automated trial timing and depth verification
  • Extend depth range to 15m with longer measurement intervals (10min vs. 3min)
  • Test in saltwater environments and explore radiation detection applications near hydrothermal vents
  • Implement gyroscope-based tilt correction in post-processing to account for non-vertical orientations

  • Rapid iteration when designs didn't meet field conditions
  • Risk mitigation through redundant systems (nested waterproofing, tether backup)
  • Data analysis to identify root causes of systematic errors
  • Technical communication: Authored comprehensive research paper documenting methodology for class
  • Roadmap planning based on failure mode analysis experiment results
