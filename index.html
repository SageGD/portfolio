From Scrap to Profit

AI-Powered Copper Scrap Sorting

Product Strategy Stakeholder Management ROI Analysis Requirements Definition

Built AI-powered sensor system targeting 30% cost reduction. Delivered 88.75% accuracy through scrap yard visits, ROI analysis, and relentless iteration.

Opportunity

  • Problem: Manual copper sorting reduces profit with high operational costs and inconsistent quality
  • Hypothesis: Automated sensor fusion + AI can reduce labor dependency while improving accuracy
  • Success metric: 88.75% classification accuracy on high-grade copper

User Research

  • Recyclers: Need faster throughput, reduced labor costs, higher grade separation accuracy
  • Conducted scrap yard site visit to understand operational constraints and pain points

Product Requirements

  • Sensor stack: Spectroscopy, 3D depth camera, weight sensors, multiple visual cameras, object detection
  • System integration: Automated conveyor triggering sensors at detection points
  • ML pipeline: Multi-modal model trained on spectral, weight, geometry, and visual features
  • Performance: 88.75% accuracy on three-fold cross-validation for high-grade copper

Data-Driven Decisions

  • ROI analysis: Identified optical imaging as highest-value sensor for MVP
  • Error analysis: Paint/coatings identified as primary failure mode
  • Recommendation: Add thermal, multispectral, and polarized imaging in V2 to address errors

Business Impact

  • Demonstrated potential to reduce sorting labor costs and increase scrap recovery
  • Validated product-market fit through industrial partner collaboration
  • Presented findings at MIT Energy Initiative Annual Research Conference

Demonstrated PM Skills

  • Scoped cost-effective MVP
  • Created product roadmap informed by failure mode analysis and market needs
  • Communicated technical tradeoffs to engineers and lab leadership

Worked to Death

Innovative End-of-Life Care

User Research Persona Development Co-Design Service Design

Spent 3 months in research. 10+ interviews revealed burnout, family conflict, and systemic isolation. Co-designed Death BnB: nonprofit lodges bringing community back to American death culture.

Problem & Opportunity

  • Problem: US death culture lacks community support systems present in global deathcare traditions
  • User pain point: Doulas experience burnout from emotional burden and family conflict management
  • Market gap: No community-focused alternative to medicalized hospice model
  • Opportunity to "de-corporatize" end-of-life care

User Research

  • Conducted 10 stakeholder interactions with team: 4 in-person, 6 remote interviews
  • Research methods: Co-designs, semi-structured interviews, observational shadowing
  • Created 10 individual people portraits with quotes, photos, personality analysis

Data Synthesis

  • Synthesized research into 3 distinct personas representing user motivations:
  • Aiding Alice: High-volume healthcare worker motivated by helping many people, struggles with burnout and lack of peer support
  • Visionary Vanessa: Focused on systemic change in US death culture, politically engaged, values community-oriented care models
  • Mending Mary: Personal loss survivor using doulaing to heal, runs support groups, destigmatizes death for broader community
  • Mapped personas on 2x2 frameworks: spiritual-political vs. frustrated-content showed strong segmentation

Key Insights

  • Insight 1: Doulas venerate global deathcare traditions that provide community at EOL
  • Insight 2: Most doula stress comes from family conflict, not the dying client

Ideation & Co-Design Process

  • Generated 100+ ideas plotted on "fanciful horizon" boldness framework and conducted 5 co-design sessions
  • Key learning: Boldness comes from cultural shifts, not radical technology
  • Validated assumptions, iterated, and refined ideas to 3 final concepts

Solution: Death BnB Value Proposition

  • What it is: Nonprofit network of lodges providing community-centered end-of-life care
  • Different from hospice: Focuses on quality of life and community vs. medical care and life extension
  • For doulas: Creates professional network for experience-sharing and reduces isolation
  • For clients: Scenic locations with peers, meaningful activities, and holistic support
  • For families: Reduces trauma of home death by providing community support
  • Funding model: 501(c)(3) via donations and service charges

Impact

  • Validated concept addressing critical gap in US healthcare system
  • Designed solution serving all three personas with distinct value props:
  • Created compelling vision of transformed death culture in US

The Goldilocks Box

Underwater Muon Detection System

Hardware Product Development Rapid Prototyping Constraint-Based Design Data Analysis

Built three boxes to solve it. First: buoyant IP68 housing that wouldn't sink. Second: lead-brick fortress that wouldn't come up. Third: lightweight dry bag with measured ballast. Found a solution to this Goldilocks engineering problem where constraints met.

Product Requirements

  • Objective: Measure attenuation of muon flux at depths 0-10m in lake environment
  • Constraints: Waterproof to 10m, lightweight enough for 2-person canoe deployment, <$500 budget
  • User need: Simple deployment/retrieval process operable in cold weather conditions

Rapid Iteration Through Failure

  • V1 (Failed): Large pressure-rated container (35.6 x 35.6 x 18.3 cm) with tile ballast - too buoyant to sink despite initial tests
  • V2 (Failed): Added lead bricks (27kg total) - solved buoyancy but now too heavy to retrieve from canoe
  • V3 (Shipped): Pivoted to lightweight dry bag and Ziplocks + 2.3kg aluminum/stone ballast
  • Made kill-decision on sunk cost ($200+ housing investment) to deliver working product on timeline

Technical Problem-Solving

  • Waterproofing: Implemented redundancy validated through testing (nested Ziploc bags + dry bag)
  • Data optimization: Analyzed acceptance angle vs. count rate tradeoff, chose 2.5cm separation for 70% sky coverage
  • Stability: Designed ballast block to prevent dry bag puncture and keep detector vertical

Data-Driven Decisions

  • Measured 70% muon attenuation at 9.75m depth, demonstrating exponential decay pattern
  • Root cause analysis: Experimental counts 55% lower than theoretical predictions, traced to wide acceptance angle (63.4°)
  • Quantified error sources: timing error <0.16%, angle error ~1%, temperature effects negligible

Outcomes

  • Delivered working underwater detector system within 6-week timeline and $500 budget
  • Balanced competing constraints: waterproofing, weight, cost, safety, data quality, and timeline

Future Recommendations

  • Increase detector separation to 8cm to reduce acceptance angle and improve data accuracy
  • Add pressure sensor for automated trial timing and depth verification

PM Skills Demonstrated

  • Requirements definition under conflicting constraints
  • Rapid iteration and fail-fast mentality when designs didn't meet field conditions
  • Risk mitigation through redundant systems (nested waterproofing, tether backup)
  • Technical communication: Co-authored comprehensive research paper documenting methodology for future classes
